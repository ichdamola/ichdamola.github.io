2Big ideas, doodled

Six concepts that show up over and over. Get these into your bones and the rest gets easier.

① The CIA Triad — every decision comes back to this

Every security control you'll ever build or break protects one of three things: keeping secrets secret, keeping data honest, or keeping systems reachable.

Confidentiality = only the right eyes see it.
Integrity = no one changed it behind your back.
Availability = it works when you need it.

Rule of thumb Encryption protects C. Hashing/signing protects I. Backups + redundancy protect A. If a control doesn't map to one, ask why it exists.

drop any leg — the whole thing falls over

② The TCP handshake — how every connection starts

three tiny packets. every internet connection ever, starts here.

Two computers can't just start yelling data at each other. They shake hands first: SYN, SYN-ACK, ACK. Once you can read this in Wireshark, packet captures stop being magic.

Half-open connections, SYN floods, port scans, most reconnaissance techniques — they all live in this handshake or its close cousins. It's why we learn it Week 2.

Try this Open Wireshark on your host. Load any website. Filter for tcp.flags.syn == 1. Every handshake in your life, visible.

③ Symmetric vs Asymmetric — the two shapes of encryption

Symmetric = one shared secret. Fast, simple, and useless if you can't share the key safely.

Asymmetric = a matched pair. Lock with the public one, unlock only with the private one. Slower, but solves the "how do we agree on a secret before we can talk securely" problem.

TLS uses both: asymmetric to agree on a session key, then symmetric to actually move data. Best of both worlds.

Mnemonic Public key = padlock (anyone can snap it shut). Private key = the only key that opens it (stays with you, always).

two different problems, two different shapes of solution

④ The attack methodology — how pros work

skip a step in real work → you get caught, or you miss what matters

Amateurs jump straight to "run the exploit." Pros walk this loop, patiently. Week 6 is where we practice each stage on a target you're allowed to hit.

The two least glamorous steps — recon and report — are actually where most professional value lives. Anyone can copy-paste from Metasploit. Not everyone can hand a client a report they can act on.

Uncomfortable truth In real engagements, ~70% of your time is recon + reporting. ~30% is the "hacking" TV shows love. Get comfortable with that ratio early.

3The big pivot: no local lab

You told me system resources are tight. Good news: we don't need them. Here's what changed:

same skills, same tools — someone else's servers doing the heavy lifting

Your new setup, ranked

Primary: TryHackMe Premium (annual) ~$10.50/month if paid yearly. Browser-based Kali (the "AttackBox") with everything pre-installed. Covers ~80% of the curriculum out of the box.
Free supplement #1: PortSwigger Web Security Academy Best-in-class for Week 5 (web security). Completely free, no install, browser-based. Used by working pentesters. Bookmark it.
Free supplement #2: OverTheWire (Bandit) Just needs an SSH client — even a phone can do it. Perfect for Week 3 Linux practice.
Skip for now: Hack The Box More expensive, steeper learning curve. Great for later. Not needed for this course.
